01

三种名字

药名看起来“奇怪”,是因为它们不是随便起的,而是按一套国际规则拼出来的。一个药通常有三种名字:

1 · 化学名

按分子结构命名,例如 N-acetyl-para-aminophenol,又长又难记,只在化学和研发阶段使用。

2 · 通用名(INN)

国际非专利药名,由世界卫生组织(WHO)统一审定,全球通用,也就是说明书上的“成分名”,如 acetaminophen / paracetamol(对乙酰氨基酚)。

3 · 商品名

药企注册的品牌名,如 Tylenol(泰诺)、Panadol(必理痛)。同一个成分可以有很多商品名。

02

词干:通用名的核心规则

INN 最关键的设计是用词尾(或词中)表示药物类别。懂了词干,大部分药名就能“读”出功能。

03

单抗的中缀

单克隆抗体(-mab)的名字里还有中缀,分别表示来源和靶点。例如 pembrolizumab = pembro + li(免疫靶点)+ zu(人源化)+ mab。

中缀含义
-o-小鼠源
-xi-嵌合
-zu-人源化
-u-全人源
靶点中缀
中缀含义
-li-免疫系统
-ki-白细胞介素
-tu- / -tum-肿瘤

注意:WHO 自 2021 年起改革了单抗命名,新名字不再沿用来源和靶点中缀,而改用 -tug(未修饰)、-bart(经工程改造)、-mig(多特异性)、-ment(片段)等新词干。所以上表适用于存量药物(如 Top 20 里的 7 个单抗),新上市的抗体看起来会不一样。
04

为什么看起来“奇怪”

词干 + 随机前缀

前面的部分往往没有含义,只要独特、好读、不和现有药重名就行。

避免混淆

药名必须在不同语言里都好发音,手写和口头也不易混淆(防止用错药),所以会刻意避开相近的拼写。

字母偏好

常用 x、z、q、v 等字母,辨识度高,听起来有“科技感”,也容易注册商标。

来源杂糅

有些来自希腊/拉丁词根,有些来自化学基团,有些是缩写拼接。

05

商品名的命名逻辑

商品名更像品牌营销,通常想传达下面几类信息。以下由来多为外界的常见说法,并非都有公司官方确认。

功效暗示

Viagra(常见说法:vigor + Niagara 的活力感)、Lipitor(lipid + 词尾)。

好记、好听

Zoloft、Xanax、Prozac。

从成分缩写来

Tylenol 来自 N-acetyl-para-aminophenol。

受监管约束

不能暗示夸大疗效,也不能与现有药名过于相近,FDA、EMA 等监管机构会审查。

01

Three Kinds of Names

Drug names look strange because they are not chosen at random: they follow an international rulebook. A drug usually has three names:

1 · Chemical name

Describes the molecular structure, for example N-acetyl-para-aminophenol. Long and hard to remember, used mainly in chemistry and R&D.

2 · Generic name (INN)

International Nonproprietary Name, assigned by the World Health Organization (WHO) and used worldwide. It is the “active ingredient” on the label, e.g. acetaminophen / paracetamol.

3 · Brand name

The trade name registered by the manufacturer, such as Tylenol or Panadol. One ingredient can carry many brand names.

02

04

Why Names Look Odd

Stem plus a random prefix

The front part usually carries no meaning. It only has to be distinctive, easy to say and different from existing drugs.

Avoiding confusion

Names must be pronounceable across languages and hard to mix up in handwriting or speech (to prevent medication errors), so similar spellings are deliberately avoided.

Letter preferences

Letters like x, z, q and v are common: they are distinctive, sound “high-tech” and are easier to trademark.

Mixed origins

Some come from Greek or Latin roots, some from chemical groups, some are abbreviation blends.

05

How Brand Names Are Built

Brand names work more like marketing. They usually aim to convey one of the following. Many of the origin stories below are common interpretations rather than confirmed by the companies.

Implied benefit

Viagra (commonly said to blend vigor and Niagara), Lipitor (lipid plus a suffix).

Memorable and pleasant

Zoloft, Xanax, Prozac.

Derived from the ingredient

Tylenol comes from N-acetyl-para-aminophenol.

Regulated

They may not overstate efficacy or be too close to an existing drug name; the FDA, EMA and others review them.
